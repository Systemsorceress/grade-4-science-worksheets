Section 1 • Living Things & Their Habitats

What Is a Habitat?

A habitat is an area where animals and plants live. Habitats usually provide the things living things need to survive.

🌱

Plants

Plants need suitable conditions such as light, water, space and nutrients.
🐦

Animals

Animals need things such as food, water, shelter and space.
💧

Water

Water is essential for living things. Different habitats provide it in different ways.
☀️

Light

Plants need light to make their food. Animals may also depend on plants.
🍃

Food

Habitats can provide plants or other organisms that animals can eat.
🏠

Shelter

Shelter can protect living things from weather and predators.

Section 1 • Explore

Meet Different Habitats

Click a habitat to investigate what it can provide.

Woodland

Trees, shade and leaf litter.

Plants may include trees, ferns and flowers. Animals may include owls, squirrels and insects. It can provide food and shelter.

Field

Open countryside and grasses.

Grasses and wildflowers can grow here. Animals such as cows, rabbits and insects may find food and space.

Urban Area

Buildings, roads and people.

Pigeons, insects and some plants can live in cities. Buildings, parks and gardens can provide shelter and food.

Aquatic Habitat

Water environments.

Ponds, rivers and lakes are aquatic habitats. Fish, frogs, water plants and many small organisms can live there.

Section 2 • Classification Keys — Animals

What Is Classification?

Classification means grouping living things based on their characteristics.

🐕 🐈

Fur

You could group animals by whether they have fur.
🐟 🐸

Water

You could group animals by where they live or spend much of their time.
🦅 🐧

Features

You can use observable characteristics such as feathers, wings or a spine.
💡 Scientists classify living things using features that can be observed and described.
Section 2 • Learn the Tool

What Is a Classification Key?

A classification key helps us identify or group living things. It uses closed YES/NO questions.

Section 4 • Negative Impact

Negative Human Impact

Deforestation

Clearing trees from a woodland or forest habitat.

Deforestation means clearing trees so the land can be used by humans.
🏗️

Destroying habitats

Changing or removing places where organisms live.

When habitats are destroyed, living things can lose food, shelter, water or space.
⛏️

Resource extraction

Removing useful natural resources from the environment.

Extracting resources can change habitats. Using too many limited resources can cause problems.
Section 4 • Before & After

What Happens When Trees Are Removed?

BEFORE

Trees provide places for living things to feed, shelter and live.

AFTER

Animals may lose habitat. Plants may be removed and biodiversity can be affected.
